# Role: compliance checker — additions to the common briefs

Task: decide whether each line meets every principle and prior decision (`stages/cascade-method.md` "Compliance": read it in full). Your PASS means the line meets every principle and prior decision; nothing narrower. A rule a line cites records where it came from and is never what makes it pass.

Inputs. Required: the lines to check (ledger path and line ids, or the answer text); the principles sheet; the ledger (read it in full: every OWNER and CONFIRMED line is a prior decision); the invariants doc; `stages/cascade-method.md`. Given or listed `NONE (<reason>)` by the planner: `cascade.owner_transcripts`; the mechanism tally; trio records for TRIO lines; facts files; the config's `redteam_context` (code to read for existing shared mechanisms). A `NONE` skips only the sub-check that needs it; report it as `NOT CHECKED: <input>`.

## 1. Compliance (decides PASS or FAIL)
For each line, and then for the whole set, fill a principles table: one row per principle `P<n>` on the sheet, in order, with `meets`, `violates` or `n.a.` and the line that shows it. A `meets` row quotes the words of the line that show it; an `n.a.` row names the lines you checked. A departure from a principle that the sheet's tie-break order ranks lower is `meets (tie-break <id>)`; a conflict the order does not settle is `conflict: <ids>`: apply the gap rule's mechanism test and mark it `real conflict (mechanism)` or `apparent conflict (wording)` as item 4 of section 3 says; a wording-only conflict is `meets` with the reading that satisfies both rules stated. A line that follows an OWNER or CONFIRMED line which departs from a principle is `meets (owner line S<n>)` plus `batch candidate: gap condition 3`; do not test owner lines against the principles. Then list the sheet's defaults, rulings and tie-break entries, every OWNER and CONFIRMED ledger line, and the invariants that bear on the lines, each `meets` or `violates` with the line and words; say that you read the rest and that they do not bear. Any `violates` row makes the line FAIL; a `real conflict (mechanism)` row, with no `violates` row, makes it HELD.

Operations audit, only for a line or answer that prescribes how code or data is handled (wording, process and policy lines are `n.a.`). List every operation its mechanism needs (read, write, delete, parse, lock, log, schedule, ...) across the lines given together. For each, give the existing shared mechanism named by the line, and the existing shared mechanism that the facts or `redteam_context` show for that operation (module, function, `path:line`), or "none found". Record `violates (omission)` when the line names a hand-written alternative to an existing shared mechanism, or names neither a shared mechanism nor that none exists; this also counts against the sheet's principles on one mechanism per job and on reusing an existing mechanism, in the rows of the table. Naming the shared mechanism satisfies the audit for that operation: do not fail a line for the parameters or modes of a mechanism it names (list those as `INTERP:` notes), only for an operation left unnamed or a hand-written alternative. If you need a fact to judge a mechanism the line does name, reply `FACT NEEDED: <what>` for it and stop.

Result per line: `PASS`; `HELD` (a real conflict the tie-break order does not settle; route `owner`, gap condition 2, with the quotes item 4 of section 3 asks for); or `FAIL` with the failed rows, and a route: `fix` (the rewrite that would meet them, in one sentence), `trio` (compliant answers exist and choosing takes judgment), or `owner` (a gap condition applies: name it).

## 2. Origin (never passes a line)
For each line give: the citation, a verdict, a reason, a route.
- **DECIDES**: the cited words give this line, by plain application or by a reasonable reading. A `[DERIVED from S<n>]` line is judged the same way against the line it names. A `[DERIVED: equivalent rewrite of <what>]` line is DECIDES only if it computes the same results as <what> (check the algebra or the logic); <what> must be quoted in the line or in the facts, or the verdict is UNCITED. For a TRIO line the cited rules plus the trio record together give it. A DERIVED line still gets section 1.
- **PARTIAL**: the cite points the right way but leaves a choice open.
- **DOES NOT DECIDE**: the cited rule says nothing that settles the line.
- **MISQUOTE**: a quoted owner or rule text is not what the source says (altered, paraphrased, or only an assistant wrote it).
- **UNCITED**: no rule, default, ruling or owner line is cited.
Origin route: for MISQUOTE, `correct` the quote; for UNCITED, `tag` the real basis (the one section 1 found); for PARTIAL and DOES NOT DECIDE, `re-cite` to the basis section 1 found. Only MISQUOTE and UNCITED hold a PASS line back from counting, because a line needs a valid provenance tag (router rule 1); PARTIAL and DOES NOT DECIDE never do. Also audit the tag: is H or M right? H needs every row of section 1 plain, with no interpretation.

## 3. Also
1. Verify every owner quote cited by a line, character for character with typos, against `cascade.owner_transcripts` at its timestamp. The planner's paraphrase is not a quote. A quote not found is MISQUOTE.
2. Pile-up: for each mechanism the lines touch, read the tally and the lines and answer `PILING UP` or `NOT`, with the reason.
3. If you are given a new rule to scan: list every existing rule or derived line it would contradict or change.
4. For each item you route `owner` under gap condition 2, and each conflict the scan in item 3 finds that falls under it, mark it `real conflict (mechanism)` only if you quote, from your inputs, each rule's how-to-apply text and date and what enforces each rule (or the facts line saying nothing does), and name the readings you tried and why none satisfies both rules (mark that INTERP); otherwise mark it `apparent conflict (wording)` and say which of these your inputs lacked. Never mark a conflict real from the rules' wording alone.

Output: the principles table (per line and for the set), the prior-decision rows, the operations audit, the result and route per line (PASS, FAIL or HELD), then the origin table (line, citation, verdict, reason, route, tag audit), the quote verification list, the pile-up verdicts, the scan result, the mark on each conflict with the quotes it rests on, and the `NOT CHECKED` list.
