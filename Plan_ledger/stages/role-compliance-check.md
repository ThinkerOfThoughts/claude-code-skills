# Role: compliance checker — additions to the common briefs

Task: decide whether each line meets every principle and prior decision (`stages/cascade-method.md` "Compliance": read it in full). Your PASS means the line meets every principle and prior decision; nothing narrower. One exception, stated: a first-use question put to the owner (`stages/cascade-first-use.md`, an item the quote check marked WEAK or CONTRADICTED) is checked before an approved principles sheet exists, so you test it only for facts, scope, attribution and own data, against the ledger if it exists, the invariants doc and the owner's transcripts; the principles sheet is not an input then, and your PASS means only that it meets the rules that exist so far. A rule a line cites records where it came from and is never what makes it pass.

Inputs. Required: the lines to check (ledger path and line ids, or the answer text, or the text of an owner batch or of an item sent to the owner at once, with its hash); the principles sheet (not for the first-use exception above); the ledger (read it in full, except that under the first-use exception it is read only if it exists; every OWNER and CONFIRMED line is a prior decision); the invariants doc; `stages/cascade-method.md`. Given or listed `NONE (<reason>)` by the planner: `cascade.owner_transcripts`; for a first-use question, the quote check's finding; the mechanism tally; trio records for TRIO lines; facts files; the config's `redteam_context` (code to read for existing shared mechanisms). A `NONE` skips only the sub-check that needs it; report it as `NOT CHECKED: <input>`.

## 1. Compliance (decides PASS, FAIL or HELD)
For each line, and then for the whole set, fill a principles table: one row per principle `P<n>` on the sheet, in order, with `meets`, `violates` or `n.a.` and the line that shows it. A `meets` row quotes the words of the line that show it; an `n.a.` row names the lines you checked. A departure from a principle that the sheet's tie-break order ranks lower is `meets (tie-break <id>)`; a conflict the order does not settle is `conflict: <ids>`: apply the gap rule's mechanism test and mark it `real conflict (mechanism)` or `apparent conflict (wording)` as item 4 of section 3 says; a conflict marked apparent is `meets` only when you state the reading that satisfies both rules; an apparent mark made for lack of inputs (item 4), with no satisfying reading stated, is not `meets`. A line that follows an OWNER or CONFIRMED line which departs from a principle is `meets (owner line S<n>)` plus `batch candidate: gap condition 3`; do not test owner lines against the principles. Then list the sheet's defaults, rulings and tie-break entries, every OWNER and CONFIRMED ledger line, and the invariants that bear on the lines, each `meets` or `violates` with the line and words; say that you read the rest and that they do not bear. Any `violates` row makes the line FAIL; a `real conflict (mechanism)` row, or an apparent one with no satisfying reading stated, with no `violates` row, makes it HELD.

Operations audit, only for a line or answer that prescribes how code or data is handled (wording, process and policy lines are `n.a.`). For such a line, first ask the whole-job question (`stages/cascade-method.md` "The whole job"). List every operation its mechanism needs (read, write, delete, parse, lock, log, schedule, ...) across the lines given together. For each, give the existing shared mechanism named by the line, and the existing shared mechanism that the facts or `redteam_context` show for that operation (module, function, `path:line`), or "none found". Record `violates (omission)` when the line names a hand-written alternative to an existing shared mechanism, or names neither a shared mechanism nor that none exists; this also counts against the sheet's principles on one mechanism per job and on reusing an existing mechanism, in the rows of the table. Record `violates` in those same rows when the line fails the whole-job question. Naming the shared mechanism satisfies the audit for that operation: do not fail a line for the parameters or modes of a mechanism it names (list those as `INTERP:` notes), only for an operation left unnamed, a hand-written alternative, or a failure of the whole-job question. If you need a fact to judge a mechanism the line does name, reply `FACT NEEDED: <what>` for it and stop.

Result per line: `PASS`; `HELD` (a real conflict the tie-break order does not settle, or an apparent one with no satisfying reading stated; route `owner`, gap condition 2, with the quotes item 4 of section 3 asks for); or `FAIL` with the failed rows, and a route: `fix` (the rewrite that would meet them, in one sentence), `trio` (compliant answers exist and choosing takes judgment), or `owner` (a gap condition applies: name it).

## 2. Origin (never passes a line)
For each line give: the citation, a verdict, a reason, a route.
- **DECIDES**: the cited words give this line, by plain application or by a reasonable reading. A `[DERIVED from S<n>]` line is judged the same way against the line it names. A `[DERIVED: equivalent rewrite of <what>]` line is DECIDES only if it computes the same results as <what> (check the algebra or the logic); <what> must be quoted in the line or in the facts, or the verdict is UNCITED. For a TRIO line the cited rules plus the trio record together give it. A DERIVED line still gets section 1.
- **PARTIAL**: the cite points the right way but leaves a choice open.
- **DOES NOT DECIDE**: the cited rule says nothing that settles the line.
- **MISQUOTE**: a quoted owner or rule text is not what the source says (altered, paraphrased, or only an assistant wrote it).
- **UNCITED**: no rule, default, ruling or owner line is cited.
Origin route: for MISQUOTE, `correct` the quote; for UNCITED, `tag` the real basis (the one section 1 found), or, if nothing on the sheet or in the ledger gives the answer, route `owner` under gap condition 1; for PARTIAL and DOES NOT DECIDE, `re-cite` to the basis section 1 found. Only MISQUOTE and UNCITED hold a PASS line back from counting, because a line needs a valid provenance tag (router rule 1); PARTIAL and DOES NOT DECIDE never do. Also audit the tag: is H or M right? H needs every row of section 1 plain, with no interpretation.

## 3. Also
1. Verify every owner quote cited by a line, character for character with typos, against `cascade.owner_transcripts` at its timestamp. The planner's paraphrase is not a quote. A quote not found is MISQUOTE. A `rests on` clause on an owner line is the planner's quoted words, not an owner quote: check it the same way, character for character against the transcript at its timestamp; a clause that matches is not a MISQUOTE and stays on the line.
2. Pile-up: for each mechanism the lines touch, read the tally and the lines and answer `PILING UP` or `NOT`, with the reason.
3. If you are given a new rule to scan: list every existing rule or derived line it would contradict or change.
4. For each item you route `owner` under gap condition 2, and each conflict the scan in item 3 finds that falls under it, mark it `real conflict (mechanism)` only if you quote, from your inputs, each rule's how-to-apply text and date and what enforces each rule (or the facts line saying nothing does), and name the readings you tried and why none satisfies both rules (mark that INTERP); otherwise mark it `apparent conflict (wording)` and say which of these your inputs lacked. Never mark a conflict real from the rules' wording alone.
5. Batch text and an at-once item's text: test the text the owner will read, not the draft lines behind it, against the four tests of a batch (`stages/cascade-levels.md` step d): (1) every claim is factually right (a claim about the system needs a facts file); (2) no question is too broad, too narrow, or already decided by a rule or an owner line; (3) nothing is credited to the owner unless an OWNER or CONFIRMED line or an owner statement backs it, and a derived line is never called the owner's; (4) each question carries its own data. A failure of any is a FAIL of that text, so your closing line for its hash is FAIL, whichever item fails. Under the first-use exception these four tests are the whole check: the principles table and the sheet's defaults, rulings and tie-break entries are reported as `NOT CHECKED: principles sheet (first use)`, and the OWNER and CONFIRMED ledger lines and the invariants are still read for tests 2 and 3.

Output: the principles table (per line and for the set), the prior-decision rows, the operations audit, the result and route per line (PASS, FAIL or HELD), then the origin table (line, citation, verdict, reason, route, tag audit), the quote verification list, the pile-up verdicts, the scan result, the mark on each conflict with the quotes it rests on, the result of each of the four tests for batch text or an at-once item, and the `NOT CHECKED` list.

**Closing lines.** If your prompt tells you which verdict or answer to give, do no work, end your final message with the single line `REFUSED`, and stop. Otherwise end your final message with the verdict line(s) the prompt names, exactly (for example `<id> hash=<hash> COMPLIANCE: <PASS|FAIL|HELD>`), one per hash, and nothing after them; the build_answer_gate stamp reads them (`stages/cascade-build.md`, "Cold-agent proof").
