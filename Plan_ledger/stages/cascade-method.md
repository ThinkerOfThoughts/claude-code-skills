# Cascade method — the planner's procedure, and the vocabulary the level roles read

Purpose: a few owner rules, stated once, settle most fine-grained decisions. The principles are a test an answer must pass, never a justification for it: a design line is derived from a rule and says which (that records where it came from), and a cold check passes it only when it meets every principle and prior decision (section "Compliance"); only what the rules cannot settle goes to the owner, in one batch. This file holds the definitions and the planner's procedures. The level procedure is `stages/cascade-levels.md`, the build-time procedure is `stages/cascade-build.md`, first use is `stages/cascade-first-use.md`.

## Layers
The project's invariants doc (existing); then, in the principles sheet (`cascade.principles_path`, template `stages/principles-sheet-template.md`): principles `P<n>` (the owner's rules for what is being planned), a tie-break order `T<n>` (higher wins when principles conflict), defaults `D<n>` for recurring kinds of decision, rulings `R<n>` (the owner's answer to a rule that pointed both ways or to a case), and the always-to-owner list. Ids are cited as written; a split keeps the parts' ids visible (`P8a`, `P8b`).

## Derived lines
A design line derived from the layers is written `S<n>. <sentence> because <one sentence: what in the cited rule gives this answer> [RULE:<id>[,<id>] | <H or M>]`. The tag comes last and records where the line came from; it is not what passes the line. A cited id is a principles-sheet id or an earlier ledger line that already counts (an OWNER or CONFIRMED line, or a derived line that passed its compliance check). A line decided by a trio adds `| TRIO:<record>` inside the bracket. `[DERIVED from S<n>]` stays for a purely mechanical consequence of a counted line.
A cold reviewer in stage 5 or 6 reads a RULE tag as the line's provenance record (the provenance its mechanism checks ask for) only when the rule it cites is on the principles sheet and nothing in the ledger contradicts it, and it still judges the line itself against every principle and prior decision (section "Compliance"); a RULE line failing any of these is treated like an unconfirmed line.
Confidence: **H** = the line meets every principle with no interpretation needed, in the derivation or in any row of its check, and the cited words give the answer as written (or a unanimous trio in which every judge said H and none flagged); **M** = applying them took interpretation or a judgment call, or it rests on a split trio, or the direction/chain rules below apply. A line that carries a direction (an ordering, default, fallback or which-wins) is M unless the cited rule states that direction in its own words, and is read back as a full sentence on the review list (router rule 2), never among the H lines. A line citing a derived line the owner has not yet reviewed takes at most M.

## The gap rule
A question goes to the owner only when (1) nothing decides it: no principle, default, ruling or earlier owner line applies; or (2) two rules conflict and the tie-break order does not settle it; or (3) the answer would change something the owner said directly (an OWNER line or a rule's text); or (4) one mechanism keeps piling up derived rules (below). The always-to-owner list is checked first and sends its items to the owner without these tests, but only an item that changes the owner's decision of that kind. An equivalent rewrite (it computes the same results) or an item a rule or an earlier owner line already decides is recorded as a derived line, not sent. The equivalent-rewrite test applies to formulas and code, not to wording. The rewrite is `[DERIVED from S<n>]`, or `[DERIVED: equivalent rewrite of <what>]` when it has no counted line; the item a rule or an earlier owner line decides is a `[RULE:…]` line citing it. An equivalent-rewrite line is shown with the H lines for objection and is not on the review list. For condition (2), the planner tests the conflict at the level of mechanism (the mechanism test) before it is posted: read each rule's own how-to-apply text, its date and whatever enforces it (code, CI checks, hooks; get these as "Facts" says and save them, including where nothing was found), and ask whether one reading satisfies both rules. A conflict survives when no reading satisfies both rules, and a surviving conflict is posted. One that does not survive is wording only: record it as a `[RULE:<id>,<id> | M]` line citing both rules, with the reading that satisfies both in its because-sentence. For each condition (2) item it routes `owner`, the compliance check marks it `apparent conflict (wording)` or `real conflict (mechanism)` before the planner's test. An item marked real arrives with its evidence and the planner re-checks that evidence and the readings tried; an item marked apparent, or not marked, gets the whole test from the planner. In "Order of work" below the compliance check is step 5 and the gap rule step 6; earlier ledgers and issues number the gap rule 4 and the check 5.

## Pile-up
Support machinery = lines whose job is to test, calibrate, measure, queue, recover or reconcile another mechanism rather than deliver what the owner asked for. The planner names each mechanism plainly and keeps a tally in the ledger of its defining lines and its support lines by id, updated after every compliance check. The compliance check returns a cold verdict per mechanism touched: `PILING UP` or `NOT`, judged on whether the support lines have grown to outweigh the lines that define what the owner asked for (support that costs more than the thing it serves), against the owner's words and the principles sheet's proportionality rule if it has one. `PILING UP` stops work on that mechanism and goes to the owner at once, as its own message, not held for a batch: the tally, and three options: keep going, simplify, freeze. It is gap condition 4.

## Order of work
For any question or fork: (1) look it up (standing rule SR1); (2) if its kind is on the always-to-owner list and it changes the owner's decision of that kind, it goes to the owner in the batch and you stop (an equivalent rewrite or an item a rule or an earlier owner line already decides is derived instead, as the gap rule says); (3) derive it from the principles sheet and the owner's lines, naming for each operation its mechanism needs the existing shared mechanism that does it (get it as "Facts" says); (4) if the answer is non-obvious, run the trio; (5) run the compliance check, and at a level or at build time the gap red-team after it (`stages/cascade-levels.md` step d); (6) go to the owner only through the gap rule. A question is non-obvious when the rules cover it but a careful reader could derive more than one answer, or when you would have asked the owner if the rules did not exist.

## Facts
When a question depends on a fact about the existing system, brief an ordinary read-only agent (it gets `stages/common.md` and your question, no role file) to return cited facts (`path:line` quotes) from the `redteam_context` paths, and save what it returns in `cascade.records_dir`. Give the file to roles as "facts". For every operation the mechanism under question needs (read, write, delete, parse, lock, log, schedule, ...), the facts name the existing shared mechanism that does it (module, function, `path:line`), or record that none was found. A role that needs a fact not in it replies `FACT NEEDED: <what>` and stops.

## Trio
Write the options yourself (check each mechanism word in them as SR2 says) and add a last option "no rule decides this". Spawn three `stages/role-trio-judge.md` agents, foreground, with identical inputs (the question, options, principles sheet, invariants doc, ledger, facts, this file) and no knowledge of each other; a judge may add an answer you did not list, and rejects any option that violates a principle or prior decision ("Compliance"). Outcome: the majority answer is adopted (a 3-0 vote is H only if every judge said H and none flagged; a 2-1 vote is M with the dissent on the review list); three different answers, or a majority saying no rule decides, or a majority flagging `owner`, is a gap; any judge flagging `review` (or a minority flagging `owner`) puts the line on the review list.

## Compliance
**The pass rule.** In cascade mode a pass means one thing: the artifact (an answer, a derived line, a decision sheet, a spec) meets every principle and prior decision. "Every principle" is every `P<n>` on the principles sheet. "Prior decision" is the sheet's tie-break order, defaults and rulings, every OWNER and CONFIRMED ledger line, and the invariants doc. The principles check an answer; they never justify it. A cold check records, per principle, `meets`, `violates` or `n.a.`, each with the line that shows it (a quote for `meets`; the lines checked for `n.a.`). Any `violates` fails the artifact, whichever rule it cites. A rule cited by a line records where the line came from and is never what makes it pass, and a line that cites a rule it meets can still fail on a principle it did not cite. Every cold role that signs off (compliance check, trio judge, gap red-team, contradiction pass, drift check) signs off on this and nothing narrower: its PASS, answer, clean or CLEAN means the artifact meets every principle and prior decision.

**Conflicts and owner lines.** A line that departs from one principle because the tie-break order ranks another higher is `meets (tie-break <id>)`; a conflict the order does not settle is gap condition 2 (the mechanism test first): the check records it as `conflict: <ids>` and, if it survives the mechanism test, the line's result is `HELD`, neither PASS nor FAIL. A HELD line does not count and is not relayed; it goes to the owner in the batch as gap condition 2. A conflict that does not survive the test is wording only: the line is `meets` with the reading that satisfies both rules stated, as the gap rule says; an apparent mark made for lack of inputs, with no satisfying reading stated, leaves the line HELD. An OWNER or CONFIRMED line is one of the decisions being met, not something tested against the principles: a line that follows an owner line departing from a principle is `meets (owner line S<n>)`, and the departure is reported as `batch candidate: gap condition 3` unless a ruling already covers the pair.

**What the artifact leaves out.** For a line or answer that prescribes how code or data is handled, the check lists every operation its mechanism needs (read, write, delete, parse, lock, log, schedule, ...), across the answer or the counted lines together. For each, the line names the existing shared mechanism that does it, or states that none exists. An operation for which the line names no existing shared mechanism (and does not state that none exists), or a hand-written alternative to an existing one (facts, `redteam_context`), is `violates (omission)` and fails, against the sheet's principles on one mechanism per job and on reusing an existing mechanism. Wording, process and policy lines are `n.a.` for this audit.

**The check.** After drafting a level, after any step that creates or changes lines, and for every answer derived at build time, spawn one `stages/role-compliance-check.md` agent (foreground) with the lines, the principles sheet, the ledger, the invariants doc, `cascade.owner_transcripts`, the tally, trio records, facts, the config's `redteam_context` and this file. A line fails on any `violates`, and is held on a surviving `conflict` (route `owner`, gap condition 2). A failed line's route is `fix` (rewrite it to meet the failed rows, then re-run), `trio` (compliant answers exist and choosing takes judgment) or `owner` (a candidate gap under the gap rule, naming the condition). The check also audits each line's origin (is a cited quote real, does the cited rule give the answer): an origin verdict never passes a line. A line counts, and an answer is relayed to the builder, only when its latest check says PASS and its origin carries a valid provenance tag (router rule 1; for a TRIO line the trio record is its origin), or the owner has answered it. A `MISQUOTE` origin is corrected first; an `UNCITED` origin gets its real basis tagged (the basis the compliance rows found), or, when nothing on the sheet or in the ledger gives the answer, goes to the owner as gap condition 1. A `PARTIAL` or `DOES NOT DECIDE` origin does not hold a PASS line back: it is re-cited to the basis the compliance rows found. Record each pass in the ledger's Cascade block (counts of PASS, FAIL and HELD, the failed or held rows and their routes), update the tally from its pile-up verdicts, and keep the block's Principles check table current from the latest pass over the whole decision sheet. Earlier ledgers keep their old heading for these records; read it as Compliance checks.

## Owner answers become rules
Every owner answer becomes a rule, not a one-off: (1) state it as a principle, default or ruling in one sentence, in the owner's own words where possible, and check each mechanism word in it (SR2); (2) if it carries a direction, read it back as a full sentence and get that confirmed (router rule 2); (3) read the principles sheet in full and have the compliance check scan the new rule against the existing rules and derived lines (a conflict is gap condition 2 or 3), and append it with the owner's quote; (4) re-run the compliance check on lines that cite what changed. A case-specific answer is recorded as a ruling `R<n>`.

## Standing rules
- **SR1 Look it up before asking.** Before asking the owner anything, search the ledger's OWNER lines and Owner questions, then `cascade.owner_transcripts`, then the owner's own formulas and definitions, then the principles sheet. If the owner says a question was answered, that is a miss: quote the answer. When a dilemma appears while building a rule, first re-read everything the owner has said on the topic before posing options.
- **SR2 Read-back words.** Before a read-back sentence goes to the owner, list each mechanism word in it (a rate, a clock, a source, a trigger, a store) and find that word in the owner's own words. A word only the planner supplied is a fork to pose, not phrasing; delete it or pose it.
- **SR3 Formulas.** When the owner has defined a quantity by a formula, a later "how should it behave" is answered by asking what input the formula needs to produce that behaviour. A layer added beside the formula is a fork, and usually the wrong one.
- **SR4 Simulate.** Simulate any formula or dynamic rule on realistic multi-entry histories before calling it working; a single-entry run proves nothing.
- **SR5 Settled.** Never call a point, a level or a plan settled while anything under it is open, unreviewed or unchecked.
- **SR6 No pieces.** Relay nothing to the owner in pieces that belongs in the batch; status stays in the ledger and records.

## Spawning and records
Every cascade agent is spawned as plan-ledger stage 5 spawns its agent: foreground, model from `models.cold_agents` with the reason stated. Give paths, not summaries, and require the agent to read them in full. Save each agent's prompt, path list and raw output in `cascade.records_dir`.
