# Cascade method — the planner's procedure, and the vocabulary the level roles read

Purpose: a few owner rules, stated once, settle most fine-grained decisions. The principles are a test an answer must pass, never a justification for it: a design line is derived from a rule and says which (that records where it came from); only what the rules cannot settle goes to the owner, in one batch, apart from the picture check below. This file holds the definitions and the planner's procedures. The level procedure is `stages/cascade-levels.md`, the build-time procedure is `stages/cascade-build.md`, first use is `stages/cascade-first-use.md`.

## Layers
The project's invariants doc (existing); then, in the principles sheet (`cascade.principles_path`, template `stages/principles-sheet-template.md`): principles `P<n>` (the owner's rules for what is being planned), a tie-break order `T<n>` (higher wins when principles conflict), defaults `D<n>` for recurring kinds of decision, rulings `R<n>` (the owner's answer to a rule that pointed both ways or to a case), and the always-to-owner list. Ids are cited as written; a split keeps the parts' ids visible (`P8a`, `P8b`).

## Derived lines
A design line derived from the layers is written `S<n>. <sentence> because <one sentence: what in the cited rule gives this answer> [RULE:<id>[,<id>] | <H or M>]`. The tag comes last and records where the line came from; it is not what passes the line. A cited id is a principles-sheet id or an earlier ledger line that already counts (an OWNER or CONFIRMED line, or a derived line that passed its compliance check). A line decided by a trio adds `| TRIO:<record>` inside the bracket. `[DERIVED from S<n>]` stays for a purely mechanical consequence of a counted line.
A cold reviewer in stage 5 or 6 reads a RULE tag as the line's provenance record (the provenance its mechanism checks ask for) only when the rule it cites is on the principles sheet and nothing in the ledger contradicts it; a RULE line failing either of these is treated like an unconfirmed line.
Confidence: **H** = the line meets every principle with no interpretation needed, in the derivation or in any row of its check, and the cited words give the answer as written (or a unanimous trio in which every judge said H and none flagged); **M** = applying them took interpretation or a judgment call, or it rests on a split trio, or the direction/chain rules below apply. A line that carries a direction (an ordering, default, fallback or which-wins) is M unless the cited rule states that direction in its own words, and is read back as a full sentence on the review list (router rule 2), never among the H lines. A line citing a derived line the owner has not yet reviewed takes at most M.

## The gap rule
A question goes to the owner only when (1) nothing decides it: no principle, default, ruling or earlier owner line applies; or (2) two rules conflict and the tie-break order does not settle it; or (3) the answer would change something the owner said directly (an OWNER line or a rule's text); or (4) one mechanism keeps piling up derived rules (below). The always-to-owner list is checked first and sends its items to the owner without these tests, but only an item that changes the owner's decision of that kind. An equivalent rewrite (it computes the same results) or an item a rule or an earlier owner line already decides is recorded as a derived line, not sent. The equivalent-rewrite test applies to formulas and code, not to wording. The rewrite is `[DERIVED from S<n>]`, or `[DERIVED: equivalent rewrite of <what>]` when it has no counted line; the item a rule or an earlier owner line decides is a `[RULE:…]` line citing it. An equivalent-rewrite line is shown with the H lines for objection and is not on the review list. For condition (2), the planner tests the conflict at the level of mechanism (the mechanism test) before it is posted: read each rule's own how-to-apply text, its date and whatever enforces it (code, CI checks, hooks; get these as "Facts" says and save them, including where nothing was found), and ask whether one reading satisfies both rules. A conflict survives when no reading satisfies both rules, and a surviving conflict is posted. One that does not survive is wording only: record it as a `[RULE:<id>,<id> | M]` line citing both rules, with the reading that satisfies both in its because-sentence. For each condition (2) item it routes `owner`, the compliance check marks it `apparent conflict (wording)` or `real conflict (mechanism)` before the planner's test. An item marked real arrives with its evidence and the planner re-checks that evidence and the readings tried; an item marked apparent, or not marked, gets the whole test from the planner. In "Order of work" below the compliance check is step 5 and the gap rule step 6; earlier ledgers and issues number the gap rule 4 and the check 5.

## Pile-up
Support machinery = lines whose job is to test, calibrate, measure, queue, recover or reconcile another mechanism rather than deliver what the owner asked for. The planner names each mechanism plainly and keeps a tally in the ledger of its defining lines and its support lines by id, updated after every compliance check. The compliance check returns a cold verdict per mechanism touched: `PILING UP` or `NOT`, judged on whether the support lines have grown to outweigh the lines that define what the owner asked for (support that costs more than the thing it serves), against the owner's words and the principles sheet's proportionality rule if it has one. `PILING UP` stops work on that mechanism and goes to the owner at once, as its own message, not held for a batch: the tally, and three options: keep going, simplify, freeze. It is gap condition 4. Post each option as numbered lines, one mechanism claim per line; the owner's answer confirms the lines of the option chosen only (`stages/stage-2.md`, "What a go covers"). Before it is posted, that text (the tally, the three options and their lines) is reviewed as `stages/review-order.md` says for text put to the owner; it is recorded as one `ANSWER` block of its own and stamped as "Record, stamp, post" in step e of `stages/cascade-levels.md` says, and is posted verbatim; it is still sent at once, after that review, and not held for a batch.

## The picture check
In cascade mode only, at the start of every topic, and for every builder question that a spec line does not already answer, Planning posts its picture of the topic to the owner and waits. It derives nothing and spawns no reviewer on that topic until the owner has confirmed or corrected the picture. A spec line that answers only part of a builder question does not answer it: the picture check runs. The picture is plain words with no internal labels and states: what the thing is, which process it runs in, what starts what, and which existing code shape it keeps or changes. The picture check is a route to the owner beside the gap rule, and the gap rule's own tests are not changed. It is not a question or a batch and gets no review (`stages/review-order.md`, "Text put to the owner").

## Order of work
At the start of every topic, the picture check comes before anything is derived. For any question or fork: (1) look it up (standing rule SR1); (2) if its kind is on the always-to-owner list and it changes the owner's decision of that kind, it goes to the owner in the batch and you stop (an equivalent rewrite or an item a rule or an earlier owner line already decides is derived instead, as the gap rule says); (3) derive it from the principles sheet and the owner's lines, starting from the existing mechanism that does the job, whole or with the smallest change ("The whole job"), if there is one (get it as "Facts" says); (4) if the answer is non-obvious, run the trio; (5) run the compliance check, and at a level or at build time the gap red-team (`stages/cascade-levels.md` step d; `stages/review-order.md`); (6) go to the owner only through the gap rule (the picture check above is a route beside it), except a disagreement with an owner statement, which is surfaced at the wrap-up of the topic it concerns, as a contact of its own and not held for a batch (`stages/stage-1.md`, "Owner statements"). A question is non-obvious when the rules cover it but a careful reader could derive more than one answer, or when you would have asked the owner if the rules did not exist.

## Facts
When a question depends on a fact about the existing system, brief an ordinary read-only agent (it gets `stages/common.md` and your question, no role file) to return cited facts (`path:line` quotes) from the `redteam_context` paths, and save what it returns in `cascade.records_dir`. Give the file to roles as "facts". The facts name the existing mechanism that does the job the question needs built, either whole or with the smallest change, or record that none exists ("The whole job" below); the draft starts from that mechanism when there is one. For a question that goes to the trio (below), the facts also name, for every operation the mechanism under question needs (read, write, delete, parse, lock, log, schedule, ...), the existing shared mechanism that does it (module, function, `path:line`), or record that none was found. A role that needs a fact not in it replies `FACT NEEDED: <what>` and stops.

## Trio
Write the options yourself (check each mechanism word in them as SR2 says) and add a last option "no rule decides this". Spawn three `stages/role-trio-judge.md` agents, foreground, with identical inputs (the question, options, principles sheet, invariants doc, ledger, facts, this file) and no knowledge of each other; a judge may add an answer you did not list, and rejects any option that violates a principle or prior decision ("Compliance"). Outcome: the majority answer is adopted (a 3-0 vote is H only if every judge said H and none flagged; a 2-1 vote is M with the dissent on the review list); three different answers, or a majority saying no rule decides, or a majority flagging `owner`, is a gap; any judge flagging `review` (or a minority flagging `owner`) puts the line on the review list.

## Compliance
**What a check records.** A trio judge's answer means the option meets every principle and prior decision. "Every principle" is every `P<n>` on the principles sheet. "Prior decision" is the sheet's tie-break order, defaults and rulings, every OWNER and CONFIRMED ledger line, and the invariants doc. The principles check an answer; they never justify it. What a review returns: `stages/review-order.md` ("What a full review returns"). Each row carries the line that shows it (a quote for `meets`; the lines checked for `n.a.`). A rule cited by a line records where the line came from and is never what makes it pass. A first-use question put to the owner (`stages/cascade-first-use.md`, an item the quote check marked WEAK or CONTRADICTED) is checked before an approved principles sheet exists, so it is tested only for facts, scope, attribution and own data, against the ledger if it exists, the invariants doc and the owner's transcripts, and its PASS or CLEAN means only that it meets the rules that exist so far.

**Conflicts and owner lines.** A line that departs from one principle because the tie-break order ranks another higher is `meets (tie-break <id>)`; a conflict the order does not settle is gap condition 2 (the mechanism test first): the check records it as `conflict: <ids>` and, if it survives the mechanism test, the item goes to the owner in the batch as gap condition 2 and is not relayed. A conflict that does not survive the test is wording only: the line is `meets` with the reading that satisfies both rules stated, as the gap rule says; an apparent mark made for lack of inputs, with no satisfying reading stated, leaves the conflict standing for the owner as gap condition 2. An OWNER or CONFIRMED line is one of the decisions being met, not something tested against the principles: a line that follows an owner line departing from a principle is `meets (owner line S<n>)`, and the departure is reported as `batch candidate: gap condition 3` unless a ruling already covers the pair.

**What an option leaves out.** For an option that prescribes how code or data is handled, a trio judge lists every operation its mechanism needs (read, write, delete, parse, lock, log, schedule, ...). For each, the option names the existing shared mechanism that does it, or states that none exists. An operation for which the option names no existing shared mechanism (and does not state that none exists), or a hand-written alternative to an existing one (facts), is a violation and puts the option out, against the sheet's principles on one mechanism per job and on reusing an existing mechanism. Wording, process and policy options are `n.a.` for this audit.

**The whole job.** For a line, answer, spec statement or option that prescribes how code or data is handled, the check names in plain words the job the artifact builds and the existing mechanism that does that job, either whole or with the smallest change that would make it do all of it. The checker finds that mechanism itself, never from the artifact's own account: from the facts, and also from the config's `redteam_context` and the code when its inputs include them. "None exists" means no existing mechanism does the job even with such a change, and is written with the search that was made. The aim is to reuse whole existing mechanisms: the artifact makes the smallest change to an existing mechanism that does the job and adds what is needed, instead of writing a new function. A new function called from inside an existing one because that function has a branch for the case is not reuse. New machinery where a minimal change to an existing mechanism would serve fails, against the sheet's principles on one mechanism per job and on reusing an existing mechanism, unless the checker finds, from the code or the facts, that the job differs and a minimal change would not serve; the artifact's own claim is not enough. The compliance check, the gap red-team (design check 11), the trio judges and the stage-5 and stage-6 passes each ask this question. A failure is `violates` for the compliance check (in the rows of the principles on one mechanism per job and on reusing an existing mechanism), a finding judged by the blocking test of `stages/review-order.md` ("What blocks") for the red-team (design check 11) and for the stage-5 and stage-6 passes ("What the spec leaves out"), and an option that is out for a trio judge. A checker that cannot read the code and whose facts neither name the mechanism nor record that none exists replies `FACT NEEDED: <the mechanism>` and stops, as "Facts" says; a checker that searched the code or the facts and found none writes "none exists" with its search.

**The check.** The compliance check (one `stages/role-compliance-check.md` agent, foreground) runs, reads what a reviewer reads and returns what a full review returns, as `stages/review-order.md` says ("What a reviewer reads", "What a full review returns"); for its pile-up verdicts it is also given the tally. A surviving `conflict` routes `owner`, gap condition 2. The route of a line with a `violates` row is `fix` (rewrite it to meet the failed rows), `trio` (compliant answers exist and choosing takes judgment) or `owner` (a candidate gap under the gap rule, naming the condition). The check also audits each line's origin as `stages/review-order.md` says ("The origin check"): an origin verdict never makes a line count. A line counts as `stages/review-order.md` says ("What a full review returns"), and only when its origin carries a valid provenance tag (router rule 1; for a TRIO line the trio record is its origin), or the owner has answered it. A `MISQUOTE` origin is corrected first; an `UNCITED` origin gets its real basis tagged (the basis the compliance rows found), or, when nothing on the sheet or in the ledger gives the answer, goes to the owner as gap condition 1. A `PARTIAL` or `DOES NOT DECIDE` origin does not hold a line back: it is re-cited to the basis the compliance rows found. Record each review in the ledger's Cascade block (the `violates` and `conflict` rows and their routes), and update the tally from its pile-up verdicts. Earlier ledgers keep their old heading for these records; read it as Compliance checks.

## Owner answers become rules
Every owner answer becomes a rule, not a one-off: (1) state it as a principle, default or ruling in one sentence, in the owner's own words where possible, and check each mechanism word in it (SR2); an answer that depends on a planner explanation is recorded with it as a `rests on` clause (`stages/stage-1.md`); (2) if it carries a direction, read it back as a full sentence and get that confirmed (router rule 2); (3) read the principles sheet in full and have the compliance check scan the new rule against the existing rules and derived lines (a conflict is gap condition 2 or 3), and append it with the owner's quote; (4) re-run the compliance check on lines that cite what changed. A case-specific answer is recorded as a ruling `R<n>`.

## Standing rules
- **SR1 Look it up before asking.** Before asking the owner anything, search the ledger's OWNER lines and Owner questions, then `cascade.owner_transcripts`, then the owner's own formulas and definitions, then the principles sheet. If the owner says a question was answered, that is a miss: quote the answer. When a dilemma appears while building a rule, first re-read everything the owner has said on the topic before posing options.
- **SR2 Read-back words.** Before a read-back sentence goes to the owner, list each mechanism word in it (a rate, a clock, a source, a trigger, a store) and find that word in the owner's own words. A word only the planner supplied is a fork to pose, not phrasing; delete it or pose it.
- **SR3 Formulas.** When the owner has defined a quantity by a formula, a later "how should it behave" is answered by asking what input the formula needs to produce that behaviour. A layer added beside the formula is a fork, and usually the wrong one.
- **SR4 Simulate.** Simulate any formula or dynamic rule on realistic multi-entry histories before calling it working; a single-entry run proves nothing.
- **SR5 Settled.** Never call a point, a level or a plan settled while anything under it is open, unreviewed or unchecked.
- **SR6 No pieces.** Relay nothing to the owner in pieces that belongs in the batch; status stays in the ledger and records.

## Spawning and records
Every cascade agent is spawned as plan-ledger stage 5 spawns its agent: foreground, model from `models.cold_agents` with the reason stated. Give paths, not summaries, and require the agent to read them in full. Save each agent's prompt, path list and raw output in `cascade.records_dir`.
